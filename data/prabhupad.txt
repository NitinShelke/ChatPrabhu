Title: Life and Legacy of Śrīla Prabhupāda

Content:
Śrīla A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda was born on September 1, 1896, in Kolkata, India. He was a pure devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa and the founder-ācārya of the International Society for Kṛṣṇa Consciousness (ISKCON), established in 1966 in New York City.

In 1959, he took sannyāsa and dedicated his life to fulfilling the mission of his spiritual master, Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura: to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness in the English-speaking world.

At the age of 69, he traveled alone to the U.S. aboard the cargo ship Jaladuta, carrying a few trunks of books and a burning desire to share the message of Lord Caitanya. Over the next decade, Śrīla Prabhupāda authored more than 70 books, including Bhagavad-gītā As It Is and the multi-volume Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and Caitanya-caritāmṛta.

He circled the globe 14 times and established over 100 temples, farm communities, and schools worldwide. His teachings emphasized chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa Mahāmantra, living a pure life, and understanding the soul's eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa.

Śrīla Prabhupāda entered samādhi on November 14, 1977, in Vṛndāvana, India.
